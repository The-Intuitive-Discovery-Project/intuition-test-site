Remote Viewing 101

Learn a structured paper-first method, receive a blind target ID, and compare your finished session with a real physical site that has already been used in remote-viewing practice or research.

What remote viewing means in this course

You begin blind. The target is a real physical site, but you receive only a meaningless ID such as RV-6047-3524. Your job is not to guess a famous landmark. Your job is to record raw information first - sensation, shape, line, motion, texture, temperature, sound, scale, atmosphere, and spatial relationships.

The target is the site, not a photograph. Photos and source material are feedback after the session. If the target is a lighthouse, you are trying to describe the physical location and its characteristics - not predict one exact photograph.

Why this is different from the other blind-reveal exercises

The blind reveal itself is not unique. Some of the Intuition Practice tools already hide a photo or face until after you record impressions. Remote Viewing 101 is different because of the method: a blind reference ID, a paper transcript, structured raw-data collection, sketches, AOL discipline, a clear END SESSION point, and source-backed feedback about a real site.

Before every session

1. Get comfortable

Sit somewhere you can write and sketch without being interrupted. Relax, but stay alert.

2. Take at least five quiet minutes

This is our beginner routine, not a universal historical rule. Breathe, ground, sit in stillness, or use soft focus if it helps. Stay longer if you want.

3. Do not force a picture

You may get words, body sensations, sounds, lines, movement, texture, or a brief image. Let the information arrive in whatever form it arrives.

4. Work on paper

The app gives the blind ID and later the feedback. Your actual viewing session stays on the printable worksheet.

Beginner progression: first contact through sketching

First contact

Write the target ID and notice what happens immediately. V2 includes an optional ideogram - one quick spontaneous pen mark. It is not a drawing of the target.

Sensory data

Record raw qualities such as color/light, texture, temperature, sound, smell/taste, atmosphere, or body sense. Do not pick from a list of possible answers.

Shape and space

Notice lines, movement, scale, separation, enclosure, direction, and relationships. Sketch quickly. This is information gathering, not an art contest.

AOL: when your mind names the target

Raw data: tall, hard, metallic, open underneath, repeating lines, outdoor.

AOL / interpretation: bridge.

Write the interpretation in the AOL area, then return to description. A label can be wrong while several raw impressions are useful.

End first. Reveal second.

When you feel finished, write END SESSION and the end time on the worksheet. From that moment, do not change the session. Then return to the app and reveal the target. Feedback notes go in the separate after-reveal area.

Do not rescue a weak session by stretching vague words into matches. Clear misses stay misses. The point is to learn your own signal, not to make every page look impressive.

How much should you practice?

Practice how you want. After your third completed session in a day, the app gives a friendly suggestion that you might want a break. It does not lock you out. If you want another target, keep going.

Remote viewing has a documented experimental and government-research history, but its scientific interpretation and reliability remain disputed. Use this as structured intuition practice, not as a substitute for evidence or for medical, legal, financial, safety, or other high-stakes decisions.
